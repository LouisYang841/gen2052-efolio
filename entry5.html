Reflection across GEN2052

Sequencing technology (Week 2) and population-genetic inference (Weeks 6–7): how each constrains the other

Concept 1 · Sequencing technology (Week 2)

Sequencing technology sets the resolution at which genomes can be read. Short-read platforms deliver cheap, accurate reads but struggle with repeats; long-read platforms span repeats and phased haplotypes, which is what telomere-to-telomere assembly requires (Logsdon et al., 2020). Ancient DNA pushes the same technology to its limit, because fragments are short, damaged and contaminated.

Concept 2 · Population-genetic inference (Weeks 6–7)

Population-genetic inference turns genomes into history. Allele frequencies, Hardy–Weinberg expectations and genetic drift describe how variation changes within populations, while coalescent methods estimate effective population size and divergence from the pattern of shared lineages (Speidel et al., 2019). The inference is only as good as the data it receives. Hardy–Weinberg gives the null expectation for genotype frequencies, while drift, selection and migration are the forces that move a population away from it.

The Connection

The two topics are usually taught as separate skills, but they form a loop, drawn in Figure 1: read length decides whether haplotypes can be phased, and phasing decides whether methods like G12 can detect selection; sequencing depth decides how many samples survive filtering, and sample size decides the power of a demographic model. Mondal et al. (2025) could only reject the ghost-population model because new genomes made the frequency spectrum informative; Pandey et al. (2024) could only scan selection because ancient genomes were pseudo-haploidised in a way that preserved multilocus information. In the other direction, population models tell sequencing projects what to sequence: how many individuals, from which periods, at what coverage.

Where I Want to Go Next

The question I want to pursue is whether long-read sequencing of ancient genomes from tropical Oceania can settle the PNG ghost-population question directly. DNA preservation there is poor, but if long reads recover enough endogenous fragments they would provide the time series Mondal et al. had to infer statistically. Answering it would connect the two topics I have found most interesting: what a sequencer can read, and what a model can prove. It would also test the technology itself, not only the model.
